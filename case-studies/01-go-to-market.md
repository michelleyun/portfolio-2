# Building a go-to-market strategy from the ground up

*Connecting product capabilities with market needs through audience segmentation, sales enablement, and firsthand customer research.*

- **Tag:** Go-to-market
- **Company:** Succession
- **Role:** Go-to-market consultant
- **Deliverable:** Go-to-market strategy & sales playbook

## At a glance
- Challenge: A useful product with no defined audience, positioning, or sales strategy.
- What I did: Researched the market, prioritized four customer segments, positioned the product, built a sales playbook, and interviewed trial users.
- Outcome: Established a foundational go-to-market strategy that attracted early paying customers, and surfaced the adoption barriers and product needs shaping the commercial opportunity.

## Context

Succession is a financial intelligence platform that tracks executive and board leadership changes in real time by aggregating information from SEC filings and press releases. Originally developed for personal use, the platform had potential to serve a broader commercial market. The challenge was to identify which audiences had a need for this information, understand their unique needs, and communicate the value.

## Approach

I combined market and competitive analysis with direct customer discovery. I assessed potential customer segments against their needs and existing trial-user profiles, then interviewed trial users who had not converted to understand their workflows, unmet needs, and barriers to adoption.

## Solution

### Audience segmentation & prioritization

Identified four priority customer segments through market research and assessed their use cases against existing trial-user profiles.

> **Visual: Ideal customer profile**
>
> | Segment | Customer need | Value proposition | Relevant capabilities |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Public-equity research | Monitor leadership changes across covered companies. | Identify developments that could influence investment research. | Company alerts, history, exports |
> | Event-driven investors | Screen for leadership turnover and corporate events. | Surface timely investment research opportunities. | Real-time alerts, executive-change screening |
> | Executive search & recruiting | Identify newly open roles and decision-makers. | Discover recruiting and business-development opportunities. | Role filters, company alerts |
> | Corporate strategy & advisory | Monitor leadership and compensation across a sector. | Support benchmarking and market intelligence. | Sector filters, compensation intelligence |

### Competitive research & product positioning

Evaluated competing products and their market offerings. Mapped Succession’s capabilities to customer needs, informing value propositions tailored to each audience.

### Sales enablement

Developed a structured sales playbook covering prospect qualification, personalized outreach, discovery conversations, follow-ups, and objection handling.

> **Visual: Sales playbook**
>
> | Stage | Approach |
> |---|---|
> | Qualify | Prioritize relevant roles, recurring research needs, and trial engagement signals. |
> | Personalize | Connect outreach to the buyer's sector, role, or a relevant leadership change. |
> | Discover | Ask how they monitor changes today and what information would be most useful. |
> | Follow up | Introduce a different value point and offer a clear next step. |
> | Handle objections | Explore existing tools, frequency of need, and perceived value. |
> | Learn | Record use cases, feedback, and reasons for non-conversion to inform future outreach. |

### Voice of customer research

Interviewed trial users who had not converted to understand their current tools and workflows, feature preferences, unmet needs, and purchase considerations. This feedback will inform future product enhancements and commercial strategy.

> **Visual: Customer feedback**
>
> | Research theme | Customer insight | Strategic opportunity |
> |---|---|---|
> | Conversion barriers | Existing subscriptions, infrequent needs, limited trial exploration, and budget considerations. | Refine qualification, onboarding, and competitive positioning. |
> | Feature preferences | Different workflows create different preferences for alerts, history, filters, and exports. | Develop audience-specific messaging and demonstrations. |
> | Audience fit | Research professionals, investors, and recruiters have distinct needs; recurring workflows offer clearer use cases. | Prioritize audiences with repeatable business needs. |
> | Product opportunities | Interest in personalization, context, coverage, and integrations. | Surface potential enhancements for product discussion. |
> | Product messaging | Practical workflow benefits matter more than information volume alone. | Communicate outcomes rather than listing features. |

## Impact

1. **GTM strategy:** Defined four potential customer segments, developed audience-specific positioning, and created a structured sales playbook.
2. **Customer insight:** Identified adoption barriers and product needs through trial-user interviews.
3. **Early commercial traction:** The product attracted paying customers, providing an initial signal of willingness to pay.
